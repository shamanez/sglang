What the scale-up changes

Per-token int4's collapse completes. On the 35B the boundary series is now +11% → +59% → +190% NLL (perplexity 6.6 → 240) with GSM8K 64% → 3% → 0%. Per boundary the damage keeps accelerating (0.21 → 0.37 → 0.51 nats/hop), which is the signature of error that is re-injected and then amplified by each subsequent stage rather than averaged away. On the 122B it is worse and saturates earlier: +421% at 3 boundaries, +495% at 7, GSM8K 0% at both.

8-bit stays free everywhere. int8 and mxfp8 cost at most +0.29% NLL on the 35B at 7 boundaries and keep GSM8K in the 96-99% band; on the 122B int8 costs +0.59% at 3 boundaries and +1.17% at 7 with GSM8K flat at 96%. Nothing in this study makes 8-bit look risky at any depth or size we could reach.

The two microscaled 4-bit formats separate at scale — the sharpest new result. On the 35B, mxfp4 and nvfp4 were interchangeable (96-99% GSM8K at every depth). On the 122B they diverge: mxfp4 grows from +2.56% NLL / 97% at 3 boundaries to +6.65% / 89% at 7, while nvfp4 goes +0.75% / 96% to +1.86% / 98% — still inside the control band. The difference is granularity within the 4-bit family: nvfp4 carries one e4m3 scale per 16 elements plus a per-token global, mxfp4 one power-of-two E8M0 scale per 32. Wider hidden dimensions mean more outlier channels per block, so the coarser block loses resolution first, and each extra boundary compounds it. The practical consequence is that the 4-bit recommendation is specifically nvfp4, not "any microscaled format".

Bigger models are uniformly more wire-sensitive. Comparing like for like at 7 boundaries, every format's NLL penalty is roughly 4x larger on the 122B than on the 35B (int8 +0.29% → +1.17%, nvfp4 +0.43% → +1.86%, mxfp4 +1.66% → +6.65%). Task accuracy absorbs most of that for int8 and nvfp4, but the trend says the safety margin narrows as models grow, and this checkpoint carries FP8 weights, so part of the sensitivity may be weight-precision interaction rather than size alone — a confound we could not resolve (see the negative results).

Latency: the prefill win disappears with depth. At 7 boundaries every 35B config lands at 181-193 ms median TTFT regardless of wire format, versus the 21-23% quantization win measured at 1-3 boundaries. With only 5 layers per stage, fixed per-hop costs (kernel launches, NCCL handshakes, scheduler hops) dominate the transfer time that compression shrinks. Meanwhile the eager 4-bit codecs' inter-token tax grows with hop count (+23% for mxfp4, +24% for nvfp4 at pp8). Deep pipelines are therefore the worst case for this feature's performance story and the best case for its bandwidth story - the bytes saved are unchanged, but on a fast intra-node interconnect they buy less.

1024 decode steps at 7 boundaries

The most extreme cell in the study: 1024 decode steps with error re-injected at 7 boundaries every step. Segment-mean top-1 agreement (128-step segments, first → last): bf16 0.988 → 0.993, int8 0.973 → 0.986, nvfp4 0.973 → 0.983, mxfp4 0.946 → 0.970, and int4 0.230 → 0.293. Every trend is flat or slightly positive — even int4, whose output is destroyed, does not decay further as the generation proceeds. Combined with the 122B probes (int8 0.944 → 0.986, nvfp4 0.923 → 0.973, mxfp4 0.868 → 0.964, int4 pinned near 0.02), this closes the question the literature survey opened: wire-quantization error does not accumulate over decode steps at any format, depth, or model size we tested. The only axis on which it accumulates is boundary count, and only when the scale granularity is too coarse for the activation outlier structure.
